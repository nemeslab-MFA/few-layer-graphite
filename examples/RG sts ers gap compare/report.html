Rhombohedral graphite · −1 → +1 band-edge gap · STM at 9 K vs ERS at 300 K

ERS–STS Gap Comparison

STM measures the −1 → +1 gap of rhombohedral graphite larger than electronic Raman scattering (Pálinkás et al., Carbon 230, 119608, 2024: +72 meV for 3 layers, +34 meV for 4). Self-consistent simulations of the tip, fitted to the measured spectra, show that tip-induced band bending (TIBB) widens the STM gap by the same amount and with the same thickness dependence.

Answer

The excess of the STM gap over the ERS gap is tip-induced band bending. With the tip contact potential and the SiO₂ trap charge fitted to each spectrum, the tip adds +73 meV to the 3-layer gap, +47 to +51 meV to the 4-layer gap and +32 to +42 meV to the 6-layer gap. The SiO₂ charge alone moves the gap by at most 4 meV. Model and measured excess agree within ±25 meV with no systematic sign; the one outlier is 3L map 280 (model +73, measured +42 meV). The paper assigned the 70 ± 27 meV trilayer excess to excitonic and polarization effects in ERS. These data leave room for at most about 20 meV of such corrections.

Method

Gap definition

Supplementary S7 of the paper: around each band edge the spectrum is fitted with two linear backgrounds, a sigmoid step and a Gaussian, all sharing the centre E0,

y(E) = y0 + s≶(E − E0) + Asigm / [exp((E − E0)/sp) + 1] + AG exp[−2 ln 2 (E − E0)²/w²]

and the gap is E0(+1) − E0(−1). The same code fits measured and simulated spectra, multi-start over E0. Windows: 3L −0.6 to −0.15 and 0.2 to 0.7 V, 4L −0.6 to −0.2 and 0.2 to 0.7 V, as in the Zenodo notebook. In 6L the ±2 edges lie about 100 mV beyond ±1, so each window is anchored on the spectrum's −1 peak (−50 to +90 mV around it) and +1 rise (−70 to +60 mV). For broadened spectra E0 lands on the steep flank, 10 to 25 meV inside the DOS peak; the offset is common to data and model.

Model

Self-consistent Hartree plus full SWMcC tight binding (γ0 to γ5, Δ′ of McEllistrim et al. 2023) in the bandbending engine, branch lateral-solver, set up as in kandrai2026_rg_tip_gating: neutral stack, interlayer εz = 2.6, a 2 nm SiO₂ trap layer with fixed charge nsub on 88 nm SiO₂, grounded Si gate, tip radius 20 nm at d = 0.7 nm with contact potential CPot, T = 9 K, WKB barrier 4.6 eV. Gaussian broadening 10 meV (3L, 4L) and 12.5 / 13.7 meV (6L), lock-in modulation of each map (2.5 to 5.5 mV). Cases: tight binding without field; SiO₂ charge only (tip-free solve); tip only (fitted CPot, nsub = 0); tip and SiO₂ charge (the fit).

Fits

3L and 4L: flat-band peak, x0(−1) and x0(+1) of the map-averaged spectrum, on a grid of CPot ∈ {−0.3, −0.15, 0, 0.15, 0.3} eV × nsub ∈ {−0.5, 0, 0.5}·10¹³ cm⁻², interpolated, then checked with a full sweep at the optimum. 6L: the published (+5) fits of kandrai2026_rg_tip_gating. Why the tip widens the gap

At the edge biases, |V| ≈ 0.3 to 0.4 V, only the flat band can screen the tip field, and it is emptied or filled within tens of mV. The stack then follows the tip potential with a lever arm of about 0.1 for 3 layers, falling with thickness. The added gap equals the change of the layer-averaged potential under the apex between the two edge biases:

Uncertainties

Tip–sample distance

The distance is fixed at 0.7 nm, the convention of kandrai2026_rg_tip_gating, and it is the largest model uncertainty. Over d = 0.5 to 0.9 nm the 3-layer TIBB spans +50 to +107 meV. The tip radius matters little (6L, line 940 geometry, CPot −0.367 eV, no SiO₂ charge; TB gap 486 meV):

Other limits

  • 3L map 280 is not reproduced. Its gap (748 meV) is below every tip model at d = 0.7 nm; it would need d ≈ 1 nm or a differently shaped tip. 6L line 514: model +12 meV above the data.
  • ERS is measured at room temperature on other flakes. The tight-binding DOS-peak gaps exceed ERS by +18 (3L) and +11 meV (4L) and fall 9 meV short of it for 6L. Corrections smaller than this, excitonic or other, cannot be resolved.
  • The flat-band peak of 3L is a peak plus a sharp edge in the top-layer LDOS, a fragile fit target; it dominates the verified rms but does not affect the gap.
